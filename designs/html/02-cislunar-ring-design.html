WHERE THE MATERIALS COME FROM

ASTEROIDS ORBITING EARTH?

Earth only has temporary "mini-moons" a few metres wide (2020 CD3, 2024 PT5), thousands of tonnes at most. The quasi-satellite Kamo'oalewa (~40–100 m) stays near Earth but orbits the Sun. None come close to the ~300 Mt this build needs, so the plan captures larger near-Earth asteroids like Bennu- or Ryugu-class bodies instead.

STEEL CAN'T HOLD THIS SPIN

At 412 m/s a spinning hoop must carry ρv² of its own mass. That's about 1.3 GPa for steel, beyond its working limit, but only about 0.46 GPa for basalt fibre, which leaves room for the habitat load.

NITROGEN IS THE SCARCE GAS

The Moon has almost none. At 0.1–0.2% in carbon-rich asteroids, filling the air needs about a Ryugu-sized body, or Earth shipments, or a lower-pressure, oxygen-richer mix.

LAUNCH FROM THE MOON

With no air and low gravity, an electric mass driver can throw regolith to the ring for about 0.8 kWh per kilogram, with no rocket fuel.
