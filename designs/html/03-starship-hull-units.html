CROSS-SECTION · 84 PANELS PER RING

One 18 m bay = 84 curved panels on one ring frame. The ring needs about 6,060 bays laid end to end. Panels carry the air pressure; the fibre bands under the keel carry the spin load.

C

UNIT CATALOGUE

H1 · STANDARD HULL PANEL

Isogrid skin, sealed edge flanges, 4 grapple fixtures (blue), 2 alignment cones. 46 per bay.

H2 · WINDOW PANEL

Same frame with 4 ports for glass inserts, Earth glass first, lunar glass later. 10 per bay.

H3 · KEEL PANEL

Thicker skin, an outside rail that clamps to the hoop bands, and sockets for floor-deck trusses. 28 per bay.

F1 · FRAME ARC

18 m curved I-beam with latching end blocks. 26 make one ring frame. ~40 per flight.

R1 · WALKER ARM

~18 m, 7 joints, a grapple at each end so it can walk end over end between panel fixtures, like the ISS's Canadarm2.

D

ROBOTIC ASSEMBLY · ONE BAY

1 · DOCK

Refuelled Starship arrives and docks at the bay factory, a jig the size of the tube's end.

2 · UNLOAD

A walker arm grapples the top panel through the cargo door and hands it out, one at a time.

3 · CLOSE THE FRAME

26 frame arcs latch end to end on the jig to form a 150 m ring.

4 · HANG PANELS

Two arms per panel: one holds, one guides the cones home, then latches clamp it to the frame.

5 · WELD AND TEST

A crawler friction-stir welds each seam. Inflatable end bulkheads let the bay be leak-tested on its own.

6 · ADVANCE

The jig steps 18 m forward. Six factories at the spokes build both ways, 12 fronts that meet and close the ring, then spin up.
