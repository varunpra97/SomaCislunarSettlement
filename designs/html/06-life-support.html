A

HOW THE RING STAYS ALIVE · THE LOOPS

ATMOSPHERE

57 kPa · 34% O₂

NASA's exploration atmosphere. Oxygen feels like living at ~750 m altitude. Rest is N₂ (~0.57 Mt), CO₂ under 0.1%, 40–60% humidity, 20–24 °C. Water boils at ~85 °C, so pressure cookers are standard.

LIGHT AND THE DAY

Mirrors outside the shield steer sunlight through the chevron baffle into each window strip. Louvres open at dawn and close at dusk on a 24-hour clock. Light pipes carry daylight down into the canyons and farm tiers; LEDs cover nights and the deepest tiers.

HEAT AND POWER

~5–6 GW of solar on the non-spinning hub feeds power down the spokes; farm lighting is the biggest user. About 10 GW of heat leaves through ~12 km² of radiator fins along the six spokes.

WHERE TOP-UPS COME FROM

Leaks and materials locked into buildings slowly drain the loops. Nitrogen and water come from polar ice (ammonia + water); oxygen from refining regolith; potassium and phosphorus from the Moon's KREEP rocks.

WHEN THINGS GO WRONG

606 sealed cells. Neighbouring district plants can carry each other. 30 days of oxygen and water are kept in the bases and hollow hills. With 34% oxygen, materials are chosen not to burn, and fires are smothered by flooding a cell with nitrogen.

B

EACH LOOP, STEP BY STEP

AIR

  1. Plants in farm and wild cells make most of the oxygen and take up most of the CO₂.
  2. Each cell's base blows fresh air up through the floors and canyon; it returns through the plenum under the window strip.
  3. The district plant scrubs leftover CO₂ and splits water into oxygen and hydrogen.
  4. A Sabatier reactor turns CO₂ + H₂ into methane and water; methane is cracked into carbon and reusable hydrogen.
  5. Catalytic filters remove trace gases from paints, cooking and people.

WATER

  1. Condensers in every air handler collect sweat and plant transpiration, the biggest water flow.
  2. Greywater and urine go through membrane bioreactors, reverse osmosis and UV.
  3. Clean water runs through the lakes and the 109 km river, a buffer and a heat sink.
  4. Over 98% is recovered, as on the ISS.
  5. Small losses are topped up with water from lunar polar ice.

FOOD

  1. Hydroponic and aeroponic tiers grow greens, roots, beans and grains.
  2. Deep-soil top tiers grow orchards and staples under daylight.
  3. Precision fermentation and bioreactors make proteins and fats.
  4. Fish and shrimp live in aquaponics tied into the water loop.
  5. A seed bank, crop rotation and spare tiers keep six months of food in reserve.

WASTE

  1. Food scraps and sewage solids feed anaerobic digesters; the biogas goes to the air plant.
  2. Inedible plant matter is composted back into soil.
  3. Urine yields phosphorus fertiliser (struvite) and ammonium.
  4. Plastics and metals are sorted, melted and reprinted in industry cells.
  5. Anything left goes through a plasma gasifier; the glassy slag joins the shield.
